# The rank-5 exclusion of |T5>^2 by direct census

Status (2026-09-24, evening). The census ran on the pod and did not
exclude rank 5: it returned one 5-set, and that set is a rank-5
decomposition of |T5>^2 (section 8). The board now holds
`bounds/T5-m2-upper-5.json` at the Lean tier, and with
`bounds/T5-m2-lower-5.json` the cell is settled, chi(|T5>^2) = 5. The
exclusion drafts and their certificate script are deleted; sections 1 to 7
are kept as written before the run, since the argument they make is what
makes the census complete and the decomposition unique. The design and
its measurements are section 6 of
`docs/notes/next_exclusion_feasibility_2.md`; this note carries the
argument, the census as partitioned, the controls with their results, the
soundness checklist, the pod commands, and the outcome.

Notation. psi_2 = |T5>^2 with |T5> = 5^{-1/2} sum_x w_5^{x^3} |x>, so the
entry of psi_2 at (x, y) is w_5^{x^3 + y^3} / 5. The dictionary is the
3,900 two-ququint stabilizer states u_1, ..., u_N (N = 5^2 (5 + 1)(5^2 +
1)), distinct up to phase; every entry of every state is 0 or a fifth root
of unity times a column scalar. G is the unitary symmetry group of psi_2
generated by the order-5 Clifford stabilizer of |T5> on each copy and the
copy swap, of order 50; it permutes the dictionary and fixes psi_2 up to
phase. A k-set is a set of k distinct dictionary states. The board's cell
is 5 <= chi(psi_2) <= 8 (`bounds/T5-m2-lower-5.json`, the numerical
exclusion of ranks 2, 3, and 4; `bounds/T5-m2-upper-8.json`, an annealed
rank-8 witness at the Lean tier).

## 1. The argument

### 1.1 A rank-5 decomposition is a 5-set whose span contains psi_2

Suppose psi_2 = sum_{i=1}^5 c_i s_i with stabilizer states s_i. If two of
the s_i coincide up to phase, or some c_i is zero, then psi_2 is a
combination of at most four distinct states, so chi(psi_2) <= 4, which
`T5-m2-lower-5.json` excludes (and section 3's exact censuses exclude
again). So the five states are distinct and every coefficient is nonzero.
If the five states were linearly dependent their span would have dimension
at most four and would be spanned by four of them, so psi_2 would again be
a combination of four states. Hence the states are independent, the
coefficient vector is the unique solution of the 25 x 5 system, and the
decomposition is determined by the 5-set {s_1, ..., s_5}. Conversely a
5-set whose span contains psi_2 gives a decomposition with at most five
terms, and with ranks 1 to 4 excluded it has exactly five nonzero terms
and independent states. The exclusion of rank 5 is therefore the
statement

    no 5-set of dictionary states has psi_2 in its span,

and nothing else: there is no slicing, no base point, no invisible term,
no coefficient family, and no degenerate list. Fullness (every coefficient
nonzero) and independence are consequences, not conditions to check, and
the kernel's fullness flags are recorded but not used.

### 1.2 The symmetry reduction to pivot pairs

G acts on the dictionary by permutations pi_g (the generators are checked
in `symmetry_orbit_reps` to fix psi_2 up to phase and to map the
dictionary to itself). If S is a 5-set with psi_2 in its span then so is
g S for every g in G, since g psi_2 = lambda psi_2 and g span(S) = span(g S).
The census lists 5-sets containing a pivot i and a partner j with the
other three members above j; the claim is that every G-orbit of 5-sets
with psi_2 in their span contains a listed set.

Pivots. The orbits of G on the dictionary are computed by union-find, and
`roots` assigns to every state the root of its orbit; the 98 pivots are
the roots. Given S, let x in S have the smallest root r among the members
of S, and pick g with g x = r (possible since r lies in the orbit of x).
Then g S contains the pivot r, and every other member y of g S has
root(y) = root(g^{-1} y) >= r, so y lies in the member mask of r, the
states with root at or above r. This is `pivot_plan`'s `cand`.

Partners. Let H be the subgroup of the stabilizer of r in G generated by
the Schreier generators that `stabilizer_orbit_labels` computes (a
subgroup, since the transversal is cut; its orbits refine the
stabilizer's, and every Schreier generator is checked to fix r). H
permutes the member mask (it preserves roots). For a member y let rep(y)
be the smallest index in the H-orbit of y; `pivot_plan` takes the
partners of r to be the values rep(y) over the mask, r itself excluded
(the first index of every label among `cand`, which is sorted). Now let T
= g S minus r, and let j = min over y in T of rep(y), attained at y*.
Pick h in H with h y* = j. Then h g S contains r and j, and every other
member z of h g S satisfies z >= rep(z) = rep(h^{-1} z) >= j, with z != j
because the states are distinct. So h g S = {r, j, a, b, c} with a, b, c
in the member mask and above j: exactly the sets the kernel searches from
the unit (r, j). The reduction uses the unitary group only
(`antiunitary=False`); the antiunitary symmetry would be sound here too,
since nothing is sliced, but the 98 unitary pivots already cost nothing.

### 1.3 What the kernel lists, and why it is a superset

Fix the unit (i, j) and write [v] for the image of v in the quotient by
span(psi_2, u_i, u_j), and [v]_k for the image in the quotient by
span(psi_2, u_i, u_j, u_k), all over Q(zeta_5). Let S = {i, j, k, a, b}
with k < a < b in the mask above j have psi_2 in its span, so psi_2 =
sum c s with all c nonzero and the five states independent (section 1.1).
Then

- [u_k], [u_a], [u_b] are nonzero: if [u_k] = 0 then u_k lies in
  span(psi_2, u_i, u_j), so either psi_2 lies in span(u_i, u_j, u_k)
  (rank 3) or u_k lies in span(u_i, u_j) (a dependent 5-set); both are
  excluded. The kernel's member list (states above j with a nonzero
  image) therefore contains k, a, and b.
- [u_a]_k and [u_b]_k are nonzero and parallel: c_a u_a + c_b u_b lies in
  span(psi_2, u_i, u_j, u_k), so c_a [u_a]_k + c_b [u_b]_k = 0 with c_a,
  c_b nonzero; and [u_a]_k = 0 would put psi_2 in span(u_i, u_j, u_k,
  u_a) or make the set dependent.

The kernel (`cpp/src/cover5.cpp`) reduces the dictionary modulo psi_2,
u_i, and u_j over F_65521, and for every third pivot k in the member
list computes the residues of the members above k modulo the row of k,
normalizes each nonzero residue to leading entry 1, keys it by one random
functional, sorts, and takes every pair inside a run of equal keys as a
candidate. Parallel nonzero residues have equal normalized residues and
equal keys, so S is a candidate. Every candidate is then decided in the
kernel by the rank test of the augmented 25 x 6 system modulo
2013265921: S is returned when psi_2 lies in the span modulo that prime,
which an exact span membership implies. The returned list is a superset
of the sets with psi_2 in their span, up to the modular caveat of section
1.4; the batch re-decides every returned set exactly (the same rank test
modulo 2013265921, computed independently in Python) and numerically
(least squares over C), records both, and a returned set on which both
say psi_2 lies in the span is a decomposition. Accidental key collisions
(the 16-bit key at M near 3,600 members produces about M^2 / (2 x 65521)
of them per third pivot) are candidates that fail the modular decision;
they cost time and nothing else, and their count sits outside the
deterministic hash because the reference path draws its functional
differently.

### 1.4 Exactness and the modular caveat

The states are phase patterns w_5^{q(x)} on affine flats of F_5^2 up to a
column scalar, and psi_2 is the phase pattern w_5^{x^3 + y^3} up to 1/5;
`patterns5` asserts, for every one of the 3,900 columns, that every
nonzero entry is a fifth root of unity times the first nonzero entry
within 1e-6, and `T5Enumerator` asserts the same for the target. Span
membership is invariant under column scalars, so the unnormalized patterns
stand for the states. Both primes are 1 mod 5 (65521 = 1 + 5 x 13,104 and
2013265921 = 1 + 5 x 402,653,184), so each carries a fifth root of unity
(21009 and 815036133) and the reduction Z[zeta_5] -> F_p is a ring
homomorphism: an exact linear dependency reduces to a dependency modulo
every prime, which is what makes the kernel's list a superset and the
modular decision a valid rejection.

The one step that is not exact is the direction "nonzero over Q(zeta_5)
implies nonzero modulo 65521": a member with a nonzero image over the
field could reduce to a zero image modulo 65521 and be dropped from the
member list, or two non-parallel residues could reduce to parallel ones
(harmless, an extra candidate). The dropped-member event needs every
coordinate of a small-height element of Z[zeta_5], divided by a minor of
the reduction, to vanish modulo the prime ideal; its probability for a
random such vector is about 65521^{-codimension} with codimension 22 to
24 here. This is the census assumption every kernel census of the project
rests on (the H^6, H^5, and T^5 exclusions included), and it is the only
unverified step in the argument. A two-prime member list would remove
it; the kernel does not offer one, and the assumption is stated as such
in the drafts.

The decision modulo 2013265921 is exact in the other direction as well:
a set returned by the kernel has psi_2 in its span modulo the prime, and
the batch requires the numerical decision to agree before calling it a
decomposition. A disagreement (a modular accident of probability about
2013265921^{-20}) is recorded as undecided and fails the batch.

### 1.5 The projections

|T5> is a nonzero vector, so by projection monotonicity (Lean:
`stabRankP_powVecP_mono` in `lean_proofs/LeanProofs/Stabilizer/SliceP.lean`,
for any prime local dimension) chi(T5^3) >= chi(T5^2) and chi(T5^4) >=
chi(T5^3). Every amplitude of |T5> is nonzero, so any one-ququint slice of
a decomposition of |T5>^{m+1} is a decomposition of |T5>^m with no more
terms. The certificate prints `CERTIFIED chi(T5^3) >= 6` and `CERTIFIED
chi(T5^4) >= 6` after the m = 2 claim, and the m = 3 and m = 4 drafts
inherit the attested tier from the m = 2 certificate, as
`bounds/qubit_T-m6-lower-6.json` does from the T^5 one. Those cells have
product upper bounds 24 and 64 and no lower bound in this checkout.

### 8.1 What the set is

`research/t5q_m2_rank5/decomposition.py` rebuilds the five states from
the dictionary. In the board's parametrization they are

| index | k | x0 | W | Q | l | coefficient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 525 | 1 | (0, 0) | (1, 4) | 0 | 0 | w^0 / sqrt 5 |
| 563 | 1 | (0, 1) | (1, 4) | 3 | 2 | w^1 / sqrt 5 |
| 591 | 1 | (0, 2) | (1, 4) | 1 | 3 | w^3 / sqrt 5 |
| 619 | 1 | (0, 3) | (1, 4) | 4 | 3 | w^2 / sqrt 5 |
| 637 | 1 | (0, 4) | (1, 4) | 2 | 2 | w^4 / sqrt 5 |

that is, state c is the line x + y = c of F_5^2, parametrized by x, with
the quadratic phase w^{3c x^2 - 3c^2 x} (Q = 3c, l = -3c^2 mod 5), and its
coefficient is w^{c^3} / sqrt 5. The identity behind it is one line: on
x + y = c,

    x^3 + y^3 = x^3 + (c - x)^3 = c^3 + 3c x^2 - 3c^2 x,

the cubic terms cancel, so the restriction of the phase polynomial of
psi_2 to each line is quadratic and each restriction is a stabilizer
state. The lines x + y = c are the eigenspaces of Z (x) Z (eigenvalue
w^c), so the decomposition is the Pauli eigensector construction of
`research/constructions/sectors.py` with P = Z (x) Z: psi_2 = sum_c Pi_c
psi_2 with every sector a stabilizer state (norm 1/sqrt 5 each, overlap 1
with the listed state). It is the p = 5 analogue of the two-copy carry
decomposition of |T3> (three Z (x) Z sectors), and `sectors.py` would have
found it in a second had it been run for T5; it was written for the qutrit
orbits only. The five states share the stabilizer <Z (x) Z> (eigenphase
w^c on sector c) and no other Weyl operator; none is a product state, so
the decomposition is not a product of single-copy decompositions (5 < 9
rules that out anyway).

Checks: the identity is decided in Z[w] (an element is zero iff its five
integer coefficients on 1, w, ..., w^4 agree, as in
`cert_t5_m1_rank2.py`); `fit_coeffs.fit` recovers the coefficients
w^{c^3} / sqrt 5 as nested radicals that agree with them to 30 digits;
`verify_upper` accepts the witness symbolically in 0.2 s; the numerical
residual is 1.2e-15. The Lean theorem `t5_m2_stabRankP_le_five`
(`LeanProofs.T5M2StabRank`) enters the five lines as `stabTerm`s with
coefficients w^{c^3}/5 on the unnormalised terms and decides the identity
at the 25 digit strings by the tactic sequence of the rank-8 theorem
(9 s).

### 8.2 Uniqueness

The 5-set is fixed by every generator of the unitary symmetry group G
(order 50): the four nontrivial elements of the order-5 Clifford
stabilizer of |T5>, acting on the first copy, shift the sectors
c -> c + a for a = 1, 2, 3, 4, and the copy swap fixes each line. The
antiunitary symmetry fixes the set too (it sends sector c to -c). So the
orbit of the set under G is the set itself, and every state of the set has
the same orbit root, 637, which is why the hit was listed from the unit
(637, 525) with 637 as pivot. Section 1.2 shows that every G-orbit of
5-sets with psi_2 in their span contains a listed set, and the census
listed exactly one; hence, with ranks 1 to 4 excluded (section 1.1), this
is the only rank-5 decomposition of |T5>^2 over the stabilizer states, up
to the modular caveat of section 1.4 (a member dropped by a zero image
modulo 65521 that is nonzero over Q(zeta_5), the same assumption every
kernel census of the project makes). No rerun is needed to count the
decompositions: every batch record has `units_run` equal to its unit
count and the aggregate's stop at the first hit only affected the
aggregate; the 74 records are the complete census and hold one hit.

The uniqueness also has a direct reading. A Z-type sector decomposition
uses lines a x + b y = d; the cubic part of x^3 + y^3 restricted to such a
line is (1 - a^3 / b^3) x^3, which vanishes only for a = b since cubing is
a bijection of F_5^* (gcd(3, 4) = 1), so x + y = c is the only family of
lines that works. At m = 3 no plane a x + b y + c z = d works at all: the
x^3 and y^3 coefficients force a = b = c, and then x^3 + y^3 - (x + y)^3
= -3x^2 y - 3x y^2 remains. So the sector route gives exactly the product
15 at m = 3 (`bounds/T5-m3-upper-15.json`) and 25 at m = 4
(`bounds/T5-m4-upper-25.json`), and a rank below 15 at m = 3 needs
something other than Z-type sectors.

### 8.3 What it settles

chi(|T5>^2) = 5 exactly: `bounds/T5-m2-upper-5.json` (Lean tier) with
`bounds/T5-m2-lower-5.json` (the numerical rank-4 exclusion, re-derived
exactly by the k = 1..4 censuses of this pipeline, `control-rank4` and the
aggregate). Against chi(|T5>) = 3 the pair is strictly subadditive, 5 < 9,
and the per-copy exponent is log_5(5)/2 = 0.5 against the single-copy
baseline log_5(3) = 0.6826 (no exponent is published for any p = 5 state,
so the site records it against that baseline). The products give
chi(|T5>^3) <= 15 (0.5609) and chi(|T5>^4) <= 25 (0.5), both at the Lean
tier through `stabRankP_tensor_le`, replacing the rank-24 and rank-64
products of the annealed rank-8 cell. The drafts `T5-m2-lower-6`,
`T5-m3-lower-6`, `T5-m4-lower-6` and `verify_challenge/
cert_t5_m2_rank5_attested.py` are deleted; the pipeline, its controls and
the 74 records stay as the record of the census. Open: a rank below 15 at
m = 3 and below 25 at m = 4, and a lower bound at m = 3 beyond the
projection of the m = 2 cell.
